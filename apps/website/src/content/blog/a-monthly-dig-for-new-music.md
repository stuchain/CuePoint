Finding new music is the fun part of DJing, and also the part that slips first when you are busy. Beatport has more releases each week than anyone can listen to, and scrolling genre pages tends to give you what everyone else is playing.

There is a better starting point sitting in your own library: the artists and labels you already play. This post is a simple monthly routine using CuePoint's [Discover](../../guide/discover/) page, which reads Beatport from exactly that starting point.

## What Discover looks at

Discover finds new music on Beatport from the artists and labels already in your library. It reads the charts your artists made, which are short lists of tracks they play, and the recent releases of your artists and labels. It remembers every dig it makes, and it knows which of the tracks it finds you already have.

It reads Beatport as you, so sign in to Beatport first. It also works on the library you imported, so import your collection before you start.

## Step 1: set up the dig

Open **Discover ▸ New dig**. The form has four parts:

- **Genres for DJ charts.** Tick the genres you play. With artists chosen, CuePoint reads the charts those artists made in those genres. Tick none and it reads no charts.
- **Artists.** Every artist in your library, a saved list of them, or none.
- **Labels.** The same choice for labels.
- **When.** One date window for the whole dig. It starts as the last 30 days, which fits a monthly routine nicely.

A bar under the form says in one sentence what the dig will cover, for example "Look for charts in 2 genres, releases by 25 artists and 9 labels from the last 30 days." Read it before you press **Start dig**.

## Step 2: make saved lists once

If your library is large, "every artist" casts a wide net. Saved lists let you narrow it without choosing names every month.

Under Artists or Labels, **+ New list** opens a chooser. Type a name, tick the artists or labels you care about (each shows how many tracks you have by them) and press **Save**. Next month, pick the list from the drop-down and you are ready. **Edit…** changes a list's name and the names in it whenever your taste moves on.

A few lists for different moods work well, for example one for warm-up artists and one for peak-time labels.

## Step 3: let it run

**Start dig** runs in the background. The status strip shows how far it has got, with a **Stop** button, and you can keep working in the rest of the app. The page opens **Results** on the new dig as soon as it begins, and cards appear as they arrive.

If a dig stops partway, because you pressed **Stop**, Beatport did not answer, or CuePoint was closed, it keeps what it found and offers **Continue**. Continue skips what was already read and adds only what is missing.

## Step 4: listen and keep what you like

Open the dig in **Results**. Its tracks are grouped by where they came from, and each track tells you whether it is already in your library, so you do not buy something twice.

Every Beatport row has a small round ▶ that plays Beatport's two-minute sample. A double-click on the row does the same. Only one sound plays at a time, so you can move down the list quickly.

When something is worth keeping, select it and choose **Add to wantlist**. The wantlist remembers the dig each track came from and lets you add a note of your own with **Edit note…**.

## Step 5: from wantlist to Beatport

The **Wantlist** tab is your own list of tracks you want. Two filters help you keep it tidy: **In your library** and **Marked bought**. "Bought, but not in my library yet" is **Marked bought: Yes** with **In your library: No**.

When you are ready to buy, **Add to a Beatport playlist…** makes a new playlist on your Beatport account with the tracks you selected, named with today's date unless you change it. It leaves out tracks already in your library unless you tick **Include tracks already in your library**. When it is done, **Open the playlist** takes you there.

Once you have bought and imported the files, Clean matches them, and the wantlist shows them as in your library.

## Make it a habit

Once your lists exist, the whole routine is a few clicks a month: pick the lists, keep the last 30 days, start the dig, and spend the time listening rather than searching. Results keeps every past dig, so you can go back to last month's if you missed something.
